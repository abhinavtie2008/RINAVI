# Interview guide — the last real attempt

**Status:** draft instrument, 2026-11-24. 25–30 minutes. Ask before the demo,
always: an account of what happened beats a reaction to a prototype, and a
reaction given first contaminates the account (`../compliance/pilot-plan.md` §4).

## The spine

One event: **the last time this person needed an income certificate.** Not
"how do you usually", not "would you use an app that". A single remembered
episode produces dates, names of offices, screen behaviour and dead ends; a
general opinion produces adjectives.

Move forward in time through the attempt — decided, found out, gathered, filled,
uploaded, paid, submitted, tracked, collected, corrected. When an answer jumps
backwards, let it, then return to where you were.

## A. The attempt (5 min)

| Ask | Probe | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| "Take me back to the last time you needed an income certificate. What was it for?" | "Who told you that you needed it?" | The trigger — a scholarship, a school fee, a scheme. It predicts how much the person knew before starting |
| "When was that?" | "Same year as…?" | Recency. If it was more than two years ago, ask for the most recent one instead |
| "Had you applied for one before that?" | "How did that one go?" | First-timers and repeat applicants fail differently; the note records which |

## B. Doing it (8 min)

Walk the attempt in order. Do not summarise for them, and do not correct the
order — the order they remember is the order they will repeat.

| Ask | Probe | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| "Starting from the moment you decided to apply — what did you do first?" | "What did you open, or where did you go?" | Whether the first step was a search, a relative, an agent or an office. The first step is the product's entry point, or its absence |
| "Then what?" | Repeat after every answer. Five times is normal | Each hop: portal, cybercafé, notary, bank, tehsil office |
| "What did you have in your hand at that point?" | "Where did that paper come from?" | The document hunt — the step outsiders underestimate most |
| "Who else was involved?" | "Did you pay them? Roughly what band?" | Family help vs paid help, and who held the knowledge |
| "How long did each part take — same day, a few days, weeks?" | "What were you doing while you waited?" | Elapsed time vs working time. Both matter; the baseline in 07 needs the split |

## C. Where it broke (7 min)

This block carries the product. Do not skip it to stay on schedule; cut the
demo instead.

| Ask | Probe | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| "Where did it get stuck?" | "What did the screen say?" / "What did the person at the counter say?" | The exact wording, if they remember it. Ask them to say it as they heard it |
| "What did you do next?" | "And then?" | Retry, new document, office trip, agent, abandonment. The recovery path is the cost |
| "How many times did you have to redo something?" | "What had to be redone?" | Rejections and re-uploads. This is the number the scorecard weights |
| "Was there a point where you thought about giving up?" | "What kept you going, or what made you stop?" | The abandonment threshold. Ask what it would have taken to continue |
| "Did anyone explain why it was rejected?" | "In what language?" | Whether the citizen learns anything from a failure, or only loses a week |

## D. Cost, trust and what they would not hand over (5 min)

| Ask | Probe | Listen for |
|---|---|---|
| "What did it cost you, all in?" | "Fees, agent, travel, leave from work — rough bands are fine" | Bands only. Never push for an exact figure, and never write one down if volunteered |
| "What would you not trust a tool like this with?" | "What would make you close the tab?" | The trust boundary is product design input, not sentiment |
| "If a tool prepared the form and you still had to submit it yourself at the portal, would that have helped?" | "Which part of it?" | Asked last, and concretely — a yes here is weak evidence; the named part is the evidence |
| "Would you have paid for that? What for?" | "One-time or per application?" | Willingness to pay is directional only at n=20. Record the exact words, not a number you computed |

## E. Close (3 min)

- "If you could change one thing about that process, what would it be?"
- "Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?"
- "Do you know one person in a different situation — someone who used an agent,
  or someone who doesn't read English comfortably — who might talk to me?"

The referral ask is how the mix in 02 gets filled without scraping friends.

## Five rules for asking

1. **Ask about the last time, not about tools.** "Would you use an app that…"
   produces polite fiction. The remembered attempt produces constraints.
2. **"Then what?" is the whole method.** The fifth answer is usually the real
   one; the first is the summary.
3. **Silence is a probe.** Count to five before rescuing a pause. The rescuer
   hears their own hypothesis confirmed.
4. **Never correct the account.** If they say the form asked for something it
   doesn't, write it down — misremembered requirements are what the next
   applicant will also believe.
5. **Don't defend the product.** Discovery is not a demo review. If they
   criticise something you built, write it verbatim and say thank you.

## What not to ask, at all

- Aadhaar number, application or reference number, exact income, caste,
  religion, date of birth, full address, employer name.
- Portal passwords, OTPs, CAPTCHA answers — and if offered, refuse and note it
  (`pilot-plan.md` §8).
- "Can I see the document?" Never. If they reach for one, stop the interview
  and ask them to put it away.
- Leading ratings: "was it frustrating?", "how bad was it out of ten?". Ask what
  happened, then let the coding scheme assign severity (05).

## After the questions

If the participant agreed to the demo, move to `04-facilitator-script.md` §4.
If not, close with the referral ask and write the note within the hour — the
interview is worth more than the demo and must not be rushed to make room for
it.
