# Participant briefing

**Status:** draft instrument, 2026-11-24. One page. Read it aloud at the start,
or send it the day before and read the first two sections again in the session.

Plain English on purpose: short sentences, no product adjectives, no promises.

## Read this aloud

> **What this is.** I'm researching how people in Delhi apply for government
> certificates, and building a small prototype that is meant to make the
> preparation step less error-prone. Today I want to understand the problem
> first, so most of our time is you telling me about your last attempt.
>
> **Who I am.** I'm independent. RINAVI is not a government website, and it is
> not affiliated with, endorsed by or acting for any government department.
>
> **What we'll do.** About 45 minutes. First I'll ask about the last time you
> applied for an income certificate — what you did, in order, and where it got
> stuck. Then, only if you're willing, I'll show you the prototype for five or
> ten minutes. You can say no to that part and we'll still finish the hour.
>
> **Fake documents.** If we look at the prototype, the documents in it are
> marked SPECIMEN — SYNTHETIC. They are not yours and not anybody's. Nothing is
> sent anywhere and no government site is opened.
>
> **What I won't do.** I won't apply for anything for you, won't advise you on
> your own application, and won't ask for your Aadhaar number, your exact
> income, a portal password, an OTP or any real document. If you start to show
> me one, I'll ask you to put it away.
>
> **No promises.** Taking part doesn't get you a certificate, doesn't make
> anything process faster, and doesn't connect you to any office.
>
> **Notes and recording are separate.** There's a form with six tick-boxes.
> You can say no to all of them and we still do the session. You can also
> change your mind later, by email, and I'll delete the notes and recording
> within 7 days.
>
> **You can stop at any point,** for any reason or none. Just say stop.
>
> **One request.** Please don't tell me your Aadhaar number or your exact
> income. If it comes up, a rough band is enough — "under two lakh" is fine.
>
> Any questions before we start?

Then hand over the consent form (`../compliance/recording-consent.md` §5) and
read the six boxes aloud, one at a time. Do not start the interview until the
form is signed, or until verbal consent is written on the note.

## If they ask

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Is this a government thing?" | "No. I'm independent. Nothing here is connected to a government office." |
| "Will I get my certificate faster?" | "No. I can't influence any application, and I'm not promising anything about processing." |
| "Should I apply through an agent?" | "I can't advise on your application. I'm only asking what happened last time." |
| "What do you get out of this?" | "I'm deciding which service to build for next, and whether the thing I'm building is worth building. Your account is the evidence." |
| "Will you use my name?" | "No. You're a code in my notes — P07, say. Quotes appear only if you ticked that box, and even then without your name." |
| "Can I see what you wrote?" | "Yes." Show the note after the session, and delete any line they object to. |
| "Can I do this in Hindi?" | Interview: yes, if the facilitator is fluent. Consent form and prototype: see below. |

## Language

English is the language of record (ADR-0006). The Hindi layer is not built and
not reviewed, so:

- **The interview may run in Hindi** if the facilitator is fluent. The note
  records the language used.
- **The consent form and the prototype stay in English.** Reading an unreviewed
  Hindi translation of a consent form is exactly the risk ADR-0006 exists to
  prevent.
- **If the participant cannot follow the English consent form, do not run the
  session.** Record it as a finding — "consent not accessible in the
  participant's language" — and offer to book them again after the layer lands.
  That refusal is evidence for the Hindi priority decision (item 100), and it is
  worth more than an improvised translation.

## What not to say

- Not "app", not "platform", not "smart", not "seamless". Say "prototype".
- Not "we help you get your certificate". Say "we help you prepare the
  application; the office decides".
- Not "free" — pricing is undecided, and a promise made in a session is still a
  promise.
- Not "this will save you hours". You don't know that yet; that is what the
  session is measuring.
