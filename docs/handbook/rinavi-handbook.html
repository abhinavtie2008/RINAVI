1RINAVI in one page

RINAVI is an independent, privacy-first companion that helps citizens prepare and manage applications for Indian government services. It organises documents, extracts the values they contain, keeps a source-linked profile, assembles a draft application, checks the evidence, and remembers what happened afterwards. The citizen reviews it and files it themselves on the official portal.

The one-sentence version

RINAVI does the preparation and the remembering, the citizen does the submitting, and the government portal remains the only authority.

What that means in practice

The citizen getsA place where the paperwork for a government application is assembled, checked and kept — with every value traceable to the document it came from, and a record of what was filed and when.
What RINAVI gets to doRead documents the citizen uploads, hold values in a profile, build drafts, validate them, produce a copy-ready packet, and store the citizen's own notes about a filed application.
What RINAVI never doesSubmit an application, store a portal password, ask for or relay an OTP, solve a CAPTCHA, open a portal session, scrape a portal, decide eligibility, or claim any government affiliation.
What keeps it honestFour purpose-scoped consents; erasure that works even after consent is withdrawn; an audit trail that records actions but never document content; and a runtime with no outbound HTTP client at all.
Where the project isV1 is Assist-only and narrow: one service (Delhi income certificate), one geography, English, synthetic documents only, supervised pilot. Real documents wait behind a legal and security gate that is described in chapter 18.

The bet

Most attempts to "fix" government paperwork try to remove the citizen from the loop — automate the login, fill the form, press submit. That is the part with the credentials, the OTPs and the legal exposure, and it is also the part where a mistake is hardest to see and hardest to undo.

RINAVI bets the opposite: the durable value is in preparation and memory. Citizens lose time re-entering the same details into every portal, lose track of which document went where, and hand the whole task to an agent precisely because the process is opaque. A companion that explains the process, assembles the packet, shows where every field came from, and keeps a record afterwards is useful without ever touching a credential.

What "Assist-only" rules out

Assist-only is not a temporary limitation while the automation is built. It is the product boundary. It means the packet is a text the citizen copies into their own browser session; the portal is where the declaration is made; and any future step that would cross that line needs a new decision record, a new dependency, and a new permission — three reviewable changes rather than one quiet one.

2The problem

An Indian citizen applying for a certificate or a benefit usually deals with a process that is fragmented, repetitive, and judged only at the end.

What actually goes wrong

FailureWhat it looks like
FragmentationEvery department has its own portal, its own account, its own form vocabulary, and its own idea of what a "proof of income" is. Nothing carries over.
RepetitionThe same name, father's name, date of birth, address and income are typed again for every service, each time as an unverified claim with no memory of the last attempt.
Opaque evidence rulesWhich documents count, whether a photograph is needed, whether self-attestation is enough — usually learned by being rejected.
Intermediary dependenceWhere the process is confusing, citizens pay an agent or a service centre. They hand over documents and a fee, and often cannot verify what was actually entered or filed.
No durable recordAfter submission, the citizen keeps a reference number in a message, a screenshot, or nothing. Deadlines pass silently.
Asymmetric consequencesA typo in a name or an income figure is discovered weeks later, and the cost falls entirely on the person who can least afford another cycle.

Why the obvious answer is the wrong one

Modern tooling makes it technically easy to automate a portal: a headless browser, a stored password, a CAPTCHA service, a form filler. It is also the version of the idea that concentrates every risk in one place — credentials held by a third party, terms of use almost certainly breached, a declaration made by software on a citizen's behalf, and an outcome the citizen cannot see being decided.

RINAVI starts from the opposite constraint: no request leaves the deployment. No portal connection, no email, no analytics, no model API. Everything useful that can be built inside that constraint is what V1 is: verification, assembly, explanation, memory. The constraint is not a limitation to be worked around later; it is what makes the trust claims checkable.

Where the value concentrates

  • Verification — every value shows the document and the line it came from, so a citizen can check rather than hope.
  • Assembly — one draft, one evidence checklist, one declaration, one packet, instead of a scavenger hunt.
  • Explanation — the official steps and the notice that this is not a submission, in the same place as the form.
  • Memory — a frozen packet, a status, a reference number the citizen typed, and a timeline that survives the browser tab.

3Who it is for

RINAVI is built for the person who has to file, and for the moment before they decide to hand it to someone else.

AudienceWhy RINAVI fitsWhat it does not solve for them
The self-filerComfortable with an English interface and has documents as PDFs; wants the form assembled and checked before opening the portal.Anything the portal itself does; RINAVI cannot see the portal's state.
The agent-reluctantWould otherwise pay an intermediary, but wants to see and keep what is being submitted, and to keep their own record.It does not replace the agent's knowledge of a specific office's quirks; the pack only reflects official sources.
The helperA family member or volunteer assisting someone else — the packet is legible, printable and reviewable together.It is single-account: there is no delegated access and no shared workspace.
The reviewerAnyone assessing whether the claims are true: the design is narrow enough to audit end to end.—

Who it is explicitly not for, in V1

  • Anyone who wants the application filed for them. That is the boundary, not a gap in the roadmap.
  • Anyone without an English interface or a PDF. V1 is English-only and, without OCR, cannot read a photograph of a document.
  • Anyone needing a service other than the Delhi income certificate. One pack ships; the catalog is designed to hold more, and does not pretend to.
  • Anyone who needs an accessibility or mobile-first experience. The web app is a responsive PWA, but accessibility and localisation have not been designed for yet, and saying so is part of the honesty.

A note on the pilot. The first use of RINAVI is a supervised study with adult volunteers using obviously fake SPECIMEN — SYNTHETIC documents. The participants are real; the paperwork is not. That is deliberate: it rehearses the product without holding anyone's actual identity documents, and it is why the limits in chapter 17 are acceptable for now rather than hidden.

4The citizen journey

Seven steps, in the order a citizen experiences them. Each step says what the system stores and what it refuses to do.

  sign in ──▶ grant purposes ──▶ upload ──▶ extract ──▶ promote & confirm
                                                              │
        record & track ◀── export packet ◀── draft & preflight ◀┘

1 · Sign in

An email address, and optionally a passkey. No password is ever created, and no phone number is collected. The session is a server-side record; the browser holds only an opaque token in an HttpOnly cookie, and the database stores a hash of that token rather than the token itself. Sign-out revokes the session.

Stores: email, display name, optional passkey public key and sign counter, session hash and expiry.

2 · Grant purposes, one at a time

Before anything is read or written, the citizen grants purpose-scoped consent: document processing, profile storage, application preparation, and application tracking. Each is asked for separately and can be refused. Refusing one disables exactly the part of the product that needs it, and nothing else. Withdrawing later stops the next use; it does not silently delete what already exists, because deletion is a separate, explicit act.

Stores: purpose, policy version, grant and withdrawal timestamps — kept as the proof of what was agreed.

3 · Upload into the vault

PDF and plain-text documents up to a fixed ceiling (10 MiB), with the type sniffed from the bytes rather than trusted from the filename. Each file is stored under a per-account directory and gets a row describing it, its size, its classification, and its processing status. Images can be uploaded but are parked as needing attention, because V1 has no OCR — the product says so instead of pretending to read them.

4 · Extraction, with evidence

Deterministic, explainable field matching — not a model. Each value carries a confidence and a locator (for example, the line it was found on), so a citizen can check where a number came from. Documents that cannot be parsed are parked as needing attention and can be retried, rather than half-read.

5 · Profile: promote, then confirm

A citizen promotes an extracted value into their profile. It arrives linked to its source document and line, marked unconfirmed. They review it, correct it if needed, and confirm it. Correcting keeps a history. If the source document is later deleted, the value is marked stale rather than quietly left as fact.

6 · Draft and preflight

A draft is created from a versioned service pack and seeded only from confirmed profile values. The citizen links evidence to the pack's checklist groups, fills anything the profile cannot supply, and confirms the declaration. A deterministic preflight lists what is missing or inconsistent — required fields, malformed values, evidence gaps, documents that vanished, values that drifted from the profile. The draft can be refreshed to re-pull the profile when a value was corrected at the source.

7 · Export, then record what happened

Export produces a copy-ready text packet: the field values, the evidence list, the official steps, the declaration state, and a prominent notice that this is not a submission. The citizen files it themselves in their own browser session, then comes back and records the status, the reference number, the deadline and any notes. The packet is frozen at that moment, so a later catalog update cannot rewrite history.

Any point in the journey

Erasure works throughout, and never depends on a consent the citizen might have just withdrawn. A document can be deleted; all drafts can be erased in one action; the whole account can be deleted. Each of these works from the consent screen itself, which is where a person is most likely to be standing when they change their mind.

5The boundary

RINAVI's promises are only worth something if each one is enforced by the absence of code, not by a sentence in a policy. These are the prohibitions, and where each one lives.

#ProhibitionWhy it is structural
P1Never stores a portal passwordNo password, PIN or secret field exists in any table. The credentials table holds a passkey public key and a sign counter — a public key cannot be replayed as a login.
P2Never asks for or relays an OTPNo OTP field, no SMS integration, and no mobile number is collected at all.
P3Never solves or bypasses a CAPTCHANo CAPTCHA handling, no image challenge reading, and no browser-automation dependency in either application.
P4Never opens a portal sessionThe only cookie the system ever sets is its own session cookie. No portal host appears in any redirect, cookie or request.
P5Never submits an application or declarationThere is no submission module and no outbound HTTP client in the runtime dependencies. The declaration is a local boolean the citizen sets for their own packet.
P6Never scrapes a portalNo HTML parser for portal pages, no crawler, no scheduler: nothing in the system runs on a timer.
P7Never bulk-collects other people's applicationsEvery query is scoped to the signed-in account. There is no admin role, no staff login, and no cross-account read route.
P8Never stores or spreads an Aadhaar numberIf one appears in a document the line is recognised but the value is replaced with [REDACTED] before anything is saved; it is also filtered out of profile reads, promotions, drafts, validation, exports and packets.

Three more things it does not do

  • It does not decide eligibility. The pack explains what the official source says; the portal decides.
  • It does not give legal, financial or tax advice. Nothing in the product is advice.
  • It does not claim affiliation, speed or approval. No promise that an application will be accepted, or processed faster, appears anywhere in the product or its material.

Why absence is the right control. A field that does not exist cannot leak. A client that cannot make outbound requests cannot be pointed at a portal. A module that is not installed cannot submit. This is the difference between "we promise not to" and "there is nothing here that could" — and it is the claim a reviewer can test in an afternoon.

6Design principles

Eight rules that settle arguments. When two options are both defensible, these decide.

1 · Consent before processing, per purpose

No route touches personal data without a live grant for the purpose it serves. Consent is not a single checkbox at sign-up; it is four separate, revocable permissions that map to four different parts of the product.

2 · Explain, do not decide

Where the system cannot know the answer — eligibility, acceptance, timing — it says what the official source says and stops. A confident wrong answer is worse than an honest "the portal decides".

3 · Every value has a source

Values are not free-floating facts. A profile value remembers the document, the line and the confidence behind it, and goes stale when that source disappears. A draft value remembers whether it came from the profile or from the citizen.

4 · The citizen is the only submitter

Preparation ends at a packet. The act of filing, and the declaration attached to it, belong to the person whose application it is.

5 · Erasure is never conditional

Deleting a document, a draft, or the whole account must work even after the relevant consent is withdrawn. Making deletion depend on a grant would let withdrawal trap data — the exact opposite of what withdrawal is for.

6 · Audit without content

The system records what happened — an upload, a promotion, a deletion — with identifiers and non-personal context, never document text or field values. An audit trail that becomes a second copy of the data is a liability, not a control.

7 · Narrow by design

One service pack, one geography, one language, one facilitator. Narrowness is what makes the compliance story, the threat model and the test surface small enough to be true.

8 · No request leaves the deployment

No outbound client, no email, no analytics, no model API. Every feature that would need one is a new decision, not a configuration change.

7Architecture

A Next.js progressive web app in front of a FastAPI modular monolith, with one database and one vault directory — deployed, in the pilot, on a single machine reachable only at loopback.

 ┌───────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │  apps/web             │  HTTP  │  backend — FastAPI, one process           │
 │  Next.js PWA (TS)     │───────▶│  /api/v1 · session cookie · consent deps  │
 │  server components    │        │                                          │
 └───────────────────────┘        │  identity  consent  vault  documents     │
                                  │  profile   catalog  applications         │
                                  │  locker    audit                         │
                                  └───────┬───────────────┬──────────────────┘
                                          │               │
                            ┌─────────────▼───┐   ┌───────▼────────────────┐
                            │ database        │   │ vault directory        │
                            │ SQLite (dev)    │   │ /.bin    │
                            │ PostgreSQL      │   │ AES-GCM, no backup     │
                            │ (staging/pilot) │   └────────────────────────┘
                            └─────────────────┘
                     catalog/  versioned JSON packs, loaded by the catalog module

Why a modular monolith

One application keeps the invariants that matter — source-linked values, versioned templates, consent before processing — enforceable in-process, without distributed transactions or a message bus. Each module exposes a contract and is independently replaceable, so the boundary is real without the operational cost of services. In V1 there is exactly one deployment unit, one database, one API entry point.

The nine modules

ModuleResponsibility
identityAccounts, sessions, WebAuthn passkeys, sign-out, account deletion, the account export.
consentGranting and withdrawing the four purposes; the consent history that proves what was agreed.
vaultUpload, listing, reading, deleting a document; the on-disk blob store; content sniffing and size limits.
documentsClassification, deterministic field extraction, processing status, retry and the "needs attention" path.
profileSource-linked values: promote, correct (with history), confirm, reject, and the stale transition.
catalogLoading versioned service packs, serving the service list and detail, keeping packs immutable per version.
applicationsDrafts, values and evidence links, preflight validation, the declaration, export, and completion into a case.
lockerFrozen case packets, statuses, citizen-entered reference numbers, the event timeline, and "apply again".
auditAppend-only-in-practice event records with redacted detail; survives account deletion with the actor link severed.

What is deliberately not a module

A submission/execution module and a portal-integration module exist in the target architecture and are not implemented in V1 — not stubbed, not promised by any API contract. The compliance resolver, which would compute a readiness verdict beyond the deterministic preflight, is likewise post-pilot. Deferring them keeps the product's claims and its code in agreement.

Two database decisions

Local development and tests run on SQLite through an async SQLAlchemy layer with Alembic migrations; PostgreSQL is the staging and pilot database. The migration pipeline is identical, so schema work is portable, and SQLite-specific SQL is avoided on purpose. Nothing in the domain model depends on either engine.

The request path

A request carries a session cookie; the backend resolves it to a user; a consent dependency checks the live grant for the purpose the route serves; the service then reads or writes rows scoped to that user. Ownership is a dependency, not a convention — there is no route that takes someone else's identifier without a matching owner check.

Deployment shape

One machine, ports bound to loopback, the web app and the API started together. A container compose file runs the full stack against PostgreSQL for demos and for the pilot's shape, with a Redis service provisioned for a future shared rate-limit backend — rate limiting is currently in-process, which is correct for one worker and is one of the things a second worker would reopen.

Eleven tables, each with an owner and an end. The interesting part of this model is what is missing from it.

What is absent on purpose

  • No password or credential-secret column.
  • No OTP, mobile number or SMS table.
  • No portal session, cookie jar or token store.
  • No analytics or device-identifier table.
  • No admin, staff or role table.
  • No document text in the audit table.

The one exception, stated plainly

An Aadhaar number found inside an uploaded document is recognised, but not kept. The line is read, so the document can be classified as identity proof; what is then written to the extracted record is the marker [REDACTED], not the twelve digits. It is also excluded from every path that would spread it: it is not promoted to the profile, not shown in drafts, not validated, not exported and not packed. The number does stay inside the file the citizen uploaded, so deleting the document is what erases it. What survives in the database is the fact that the line existed and where — a recorded residual, not an oversight — and chapter 18 names what would reopen it.

Deletion mechanics

A document: the file is erased first, then the row is deleted. If the erase fails the request returns an error and keeps both, so the citizen can retry rather than end up with a row pointing at a file that is gone.

An account: the account's vault directory is purged first, then the user row is deleted; every table keyed to the user or their documents cascades. Only the audit trail survives, with the actor identifier set to null and no personal detail inside it.

A draft: one draft, or all drafts in a single action. Both are ungated by consent so that erasure is always available, including from the screen where a purpose was just withdrawn.

9Consent

Four purposes, each mapping to a part of the product, each independently revocable. Deny-by-default is not a slogan here: a missing grant is a refusal, and the route returns 403 rather than deciding for the citizen.

PurposeWhat it gatesWhat stops without it
documents.processingThe vault: upload, list, read, download content, read extracted fields, retry.No upload and no reading of documents.
profile.storageThe profile: read, promote, confirm, correct. Rejection is deliberately ungated.No saving or reviewing profile values.
applications.preparationDrafts: values, evidence links, validation, export, packet. The two draft-erasure routes are ungated.No draft can be created, edited, exported or completed.
locker.trackingCases and their events: recording what happened after filing.No case can be recorded or updated.

The three rules that make withdrawal meaningful

  1. Withdrawal stops the next use, and only the next use. Data already stored stays until the citizen deletes it. The consent record keeps a withdrawal timestamp, because the history of what was agreed is itself the evidence.
  2. Erasure never needs consent. Deleting a document, deleting a draft, deleting all drafts, and deleting the account are all available without a live grant. A citizen who withdraws preparation can still destroy the draft that preparation created.
  3. Rejection is a read-free delete. A non-confirmed profile value can be removed without re-reading its source or asking for a purpose — so a stale or unwanted value never becomes un-erasable.

What a grant actually records

Purpose, policy version, grant time, withdrawal time, and the account. The UI presents exactly four purposes; the API accepts the purpose string it is given, which is a deliberate, documented limit rather than a hidden one — the server-side allowlist is a known hardening step, and the public compliance documents say so.

Why four and not one. A single "I agree" would make every withdrawal a choice between losing everything and keeping everything. Four purposes let a citizen keep their documents and stop preparing applications, or keep their history and stop reading documents — which is what people actually want when they change their mind.

10Documents and extraction

The vault is where evidence becomes structured, checkable values — and the only place in the product where an uploaded file lives.

Acceptance

  • PDF and plain text. Images are accepted by the uploader but parked as needing attention, because V1 has no OCR. The interface says so rather than failing silently.
  • Size ceiling of 10 MiB per file, enforced server-side before the body is processed.
  • Type sniffing from the bytes; the filename's extension is not trusted.
  • Storage as an opaque blob under the account's own directory; the original filename survives as metadata only.

Statuses

StatusMeaning
processedParsed; fields extracted with confidence and locators. Usable as evidence.
needs_attentionParked with a note: an image awaiting OCR, an unsupported layout, a scan without a text layer, or nothing recognisable. Retryable.

Classification

Documents are classified as income, identity or residence evidence, or left unsupported. The classification is what the draft's evidence checklist matches against — a group can accept a specific class, or accept any processed document at all.

Extraction, and why it is boring on purpose

The extractor is a set of field patterns with normalisers, not a language model. It looks for labels such as name, father's name, date of birth, gender, address, annual income and Aadhaar number, and it produces three things for each hit: the value, a confidence, and a locator naming where it was found. It also tolerates the typographic apostrophe that PDF fonts produce where a plain one was typed.

Deterministic extraction is a trust decision. A citizen can be shown the line a value came from; a model's answer cannot be checked the same way. It is also honest about its ceiling: an unfamiliar layout is parked for attention rather than guessed at, and OCR or model assistance is a future upgrade, not an assumption baked into the current claims.

The Aadhaar rule

If a document contains an Aadhaar number, the line is recognised so the document can be classified, and the value is then written as the marker [REDACTED] — so it is never stored, and is filtered out of the profile, drafts, validation, exports and packets. The citizen is told, in the privacy notice, that the number itself stays only in the file they uploaded and that deleting the document erases it.

11Profile values

The profile is the citizen's own set of confirmed facts, and every one of them can be traced back to the document that first stated it.

Lifecycle

StateHow a value gets thereWhat it means
UnconfirmedPromoted from an extracted field.Proposed by a document; the citizen has not vouched for it. It does not seed drafts.
ConfirmedThe citizen confirms it, with or without a correction.Usable as the source of truth for drafts. A correction is kept in history.
StaleThe source document was deleted.The value survives, but is marked as no longer supported by evidence — and can then be rejected.

The rules

  • Only documents create values. There is no way to conjure a profile value without an upload behind it.
  • Confirmation is a deliberate act. Nothing is treated as true because a parser was confident.
  • Corrections are recorded, not overwritten silently.
  • Deleting the source marks the value stale rather than deleting it, because the citizen may have corrected it by hand and may still need it.
  • Rejection is final and ungated. A non-confirmed value can be hard-deleted without a consent check; a confirmed value leaves with the account, or becomes deletable once its source is gone and it goes stale.

Why the profile exists at all

It is the difference between a form filler and a companion. Without a profile, every application starts from zero and every correction is local to one draft. With it, the citizen fixes their address once, and the fix is available to the next application — while still being able to see which document said what, and when a value stopped being supported.

12Drafts and preflight

A draft is a service pack, a set of values, a set of evidence links and a declaration — plus a validator that tells the truth before the citizen opens the portal.

Creating a draft

The citizen picks a service from the catalog. RINAVI snapshots that pack version into the draft, seeds the fields it can from confirmed profile values, and shows the evidence groups the service requires. Two kinds of value exist in a draft: values that came from the profile (and stay linked to it) and values the citizen typed directly. The origin is stored, so the preflight can tell the difference between "drifted from the profile" and "typed by hand".

Preflight

Validation is deterministic and runs before export or completion. It reports issues, each with a code, a message and the field or group it concerns:

CodeWhat it catches
field_requiredA required field is empty.
field_format_invalidA value does not match its declared format (for example a date or a number).
unknown_formatA pack declares a format the validator does not implement — reported, never a crash.
value_not_source_linkedA profile-backed field was edited in the draft; the fix belongs in the profile.
value_diverges_from_profileA profile value changed since the draft was seeded; refresh it.
declaration_missingThe declaration has not been confirmed.
checklist_requirement_unmetAn evidence group requiring at least one document has none.
document_unavailableA linked document was deleted or is no longer processed.

The declaration

The pack carries the declaration text the official form uses. In RINAVI it is a local boolean: the citizen confirms that the particulars are true, and the packet records whether the declaration was confirmed. RINAVI never makes that declaration on anyone's behalf, and the portal is where it is actually made.

Export

Export builds a plain-text packet: the field values, the evidence linked to each group, the official steps, the declaration state, and a prominent not a submission notice. It is copy-ready, not machine-submittable. Export is rate-limited, produces an audit event, and writes nothing new to the database — the packet is a rendering, not a new record.

Completion

When preflight is clean and the citizen says the application was filed, the draft is completed into a case: the packet is frozen, the draft can no longer drift, and the locker takes over (chapter 13).

Erasure

One draft, or all drafts at once. Both are ungated by consent, so a citizen who has withdrawn preparation can still erase what preparation created — including from the consent screen, via a bulk erase that deliberately exposes no listing of the data whose purpose was withdrawn.

13The application locker

The portal forgets. The citizen should not have to.

What a case holds

A case is created when a completed draft is frozen: the packet snapshot, the packet text, the service and pack version, a status, and the fields a citizen fills in after they have been to the portal — reference number, acknowledgement number, deadline, outcome notes.

Statuses

StatusMeaning
preparedThe packet exists; nothing has been filed yet, as far as RINAVI knows.
submittedThe citizen says they filed it.
acknowledgedThe portal issued an acknowledgement.
outcome_receivedAn outcome arrived, recorded in the citizen's own words.
abandonedThe application was dropped, deliberately.
uncertainThe citizen is not sure what happened — an honest state that most trackers do not offer.

The honesty rules

  • Reference numbers are typed by the citizen. Nothing parses a portal page or calls a portal endpoint to obtain them; the app tells the citizen it cannot see what happened on the portal.
  • The packet is frozen. A later catalog update does not rewrite a past application; the case keeps the pack version it was filed against.
  • Events are the citizen's record, not a claim of truth. They are the timeline of what they reported, with notes.

Apply again

An old case can seed a new draft, so a renewal starts from the previous evidence and details instead of from nothing. This is the one action that needs both preparation and tracking consent, because it reads a case and writes a draft.

Known limit

A case has no per-case delete route in V1; cases leave with the account. The retention direction is deliberate — a case is the record the locker exists to keep — but it is a limit, and it is on the list in chapter 17 rather than a surprise.

14The service catalog

Services are data, not code. A pack describes one service: what it is, what evidence it needs, which fields the form has, and what the citizen must be told.

Layout

catalog/services/<department>/<service>/
    service.json                     slug, name, geography, department, sources, summary
    versions/<version>/service_pack.json
                                     guidance, checklist groups, form template

The catalog module loads packs from the repository at startup and serves the service list and detail endpoints. Packs live in version control, which makes every change to a government-facing claim reviewable in a diff — the same property the compliance documents rely on.

What a pack contains

SectionContent
GuidanceWhat the certificate is; eligibility notes; the official channel's steps; and the notice that RINAVI's output is not a submission.
Checklist groupsEvidence requirements, each with an id, a label, a requirement (at_least_one or optional) and the document classifications it accepts. The income-certificate pack has identity, residence, income and an optional photograph group; the residence group accepts residence and identity documents because the official source does not enumerate classes, and an Aadhaar line carries the address.
Form templateThe fields the official form asks for — name, father's or husband's name, date of birth, gender, address, annual income, occupation, purpose — each with a required flag, a format and, where one exists, the profile binding that lets the draft seed it.
DeclarationThe declaration text and whether it is required.

Versioning and snapshots

Packs are immutable per version. A draft stores the version and a snapshot of the template it was created with; a case stores the packet it produced. Updating a pack is therefore safe: new drafts use the new version, and nothing already in progress or already filed changes underneath the citizen.

Adding a service

A new service means a new pack, an entry in the portal-permission register for its official channel, and tests for its validation — not a new module. The narrowness of the current catalog is a decision about review capacity, not an architectural limit.

15Security

Security here is mostly subtraction: fewer accounts, fewer fields, fewer dependencies, fewer outbound connections, and one small surface that can be described completely.

Authentication

  • Email plus optional passkey. WebAuthn credentials are verified server-side; only the public key and sign counter are stored.
  • Session tokens are hashed at rest (SHA-256), expire after seven days, and are revoked on sign-out. The cookie is HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, and Secure outside development.
  • No passwords, ever — so there is nothing to reuse, resend, reset or leak.
  • Development sign-in is off unless explicitly enabled, is refused outside development, and is rate-limited. It exists so a local demo does not need a platform authenticator; the hosted pilot must never enable it.

Request limits

Two layers: a per-IP ceiling and a per-session budget, so one busy citizen cannot exhaust the allowance for everyone behind the same address. Individual routes carry tighter budgets — uploads, document content, exports, passkey ceremonies and sign-in each have their own — and request bodies are capped with headroom above the 10 MiB document ceiling.

Response hardening

Every response carries X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff, X-Frame-Options: DENY, Referrer-Policy: no-referrer and a permissions policy disabling geolocation, microphone and camera access. Transport security headers are set for production, and CORS is an explicit allowlist rather than a wildcard.

Isolation

Every query is scoped to the session's account, enforced in a dependency rather than remembered per route. There is no admin role to escalate to and no cross-account read path to protect.

Redaction

Logs and audit details pass through a redactor that removes email addresses, twelve-digit runs (Aadhaar-shaped) and ten-digit runs (phone-shaped), while deliberately preserving UUIDs and 64-character digests, because those are correlation keys and carry no personal data. Document content never reaches a log line in the first place.

The audit trail

Events record identity actions (sign-in, sign-out, passkey registration), consent grants and withdrawals, document uploads, processing and deletions, profile promotions, confirmations, corrections and rejections, draft creation, edits, refreshes, exports and deletions, case creation, apply-again and recorded events, and account deletion. Detail fields are non-personal: classification, content type, size, purpose, service slug, identifiers, flags. The trail survives account deletion with the actor link severed, because the record of how the system was used outlives the user's data.

Storage and failure behaviour

Documents sit in an encrypted per-user directory — AES-GCM, a fresh nonce per write, with the user and document ids bound into the ciphertext — and there is no backup. Deletion erases the file before the row and returns an error if the unlink fails, so the citizen can retry rather than being told a document is gone while the file remains. The database and the key that opens the blobs stay on the same machine, so encryption guards a copied file, not a stolen machine; no-backup is the accepted risk for synthetic data, with explicit reopen triggers (chapter 16).

Supply chain

Lockfiles pin the dependency trees; CI runs lint, type checks, tests and a production build on every change; static analysis and dependency updates run as separate jobs. Packs and compliance documents are reviewed in the same pull request as the code that depends on them.

How the threat model was built

Assets, actors, boundaries, then threats with the control that exists in the code and the residue that remains — a control with no file or test behind it counts as missing. The facilitator is modelled as an insider, because they are the OS user and there is no admin barrier to pretend otherwise. The portal boundary is drawn and left empty, which is the point: the system has no connection to it.

16Privacy and compliance posture

RINAVI is built to be describable to a regulator in a short document and checkable by a reviewer in a short session. That is a design goal, not a by-product.

The framing

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the party deciding why and how personal data is used is the data fiduciary, and the person it belongs to is the data principal. In the pilot the owner is the fiduciary and each volunteer is a principal. The four purposes are the consent architecture; the privacy notice is written in plain English for a general reader; grievances have a named route; and the retention policy is short enough to be true.

Retention, in one paragraph

Keep data while it serves its purpose, and delete it when the citizen says so. In V1 that is met by immediate, hard deletion on request rather than by a background schedule: no soft delete, no tombstone, no "deleted" flag. The consequence is stated openly — there is no automatic expiry for anything a citizen does not delete, and no sweep for leftovers. Retention automation is post-gate work.

What survives an account deletion

Only audit events, with the actor link removed and no personal detail inside them. Everything else cascades. Rate-limit counters are in memory and vanish on restart; logs are host-dependent and will be decided with hosting.

Portability, stated honestly

The account export returns the account record and the consent history. It does not yet include documents, profile values or cases. The product says so in the notice rather than letting a citizen assume a complete export, and completing it is a precondition for real documents.

Accepted residuals, and what reopens each

These are not oversights waiting to be fixed quietly. Each is accepted for a specific reason and each has a trigger that reopens it.

Portal permission, as a separate idea

"Permission" means three different things, and conflating them is how projects drift. The citizen authorises use of their data, per purpose. The portal operator's terms govern what software may do with the portal. The law governs what is required. RINAVI records the second in a register that assigns each portal an access level: A0, no connection at all; A1, text the citizen copies themselves; A2, an assisted fill in the citizen's own signed-in browser; A3, automated submission.

Every portal sits at A0. The product's highest level is A1 — a packet — and even that involves no network contact. A2 would require an approved register, a security review and a separate decision; A3 would require written authorisation from the operator. Nothing has been requested from any government body, and nothing here implies permission.

What is published, and what is held back

The repository publishes its compliance drafts: the data map, the retention policy, the privacy notice and terms drafts, the recording-consent form, the portal-permission register, a redacted threat model, the pilot plan, and a one-page brief for the reviewer. Risk categories are public; the detailed residual register and any reviewer identity are held privately. The rule is simple: nothing moves from private to public without a redaction pass, and a public version names the risk without the exploit.

The standing rule. Development and demos use synthetic data only. Real identity documents, real Aadhaar numbers and portal credentials must never be sent through the repository, a demo, or a support channel.

18The decision ladder

The order of the gates is itself a design commitment. Each rung makes the next one meaningful, and none can be skipped by shipping a feature.

The rungs

  1. A qualified legal and privacy review of the current documents — the data map, the retention policy, the notice, the register and the threat model — for the synthetic pilot, plus answers to the open questions the documents raise about consent scope and erasure coverage.
  2. Technical preconditions for real documents: key custody and backup for the encrypted blobs, retention sweeps, a complete export, a host with TLS, and an OCR decision, because most real proofs are photographs.
  3. A hosted pilot with real data, under the same four purposes and the same boundaries, with the threat model rewritten rather than amended.
  4. Any portal access at all — A2 or beyond — which requires an approved register, a security review, and for A3 written authorisation from the operator plus a new architecture decision.

Why the order cannot be reordered

Each rung changes assumptions the previous one made. Hosting adds a processor, a network boundary and TLS obligations. Real documents make encryption, retention and OCR meaningful. Portal access converts a system with no outbound path into one that needs credential handling, which is precisely the thing every prohibition in chapter 5 exists to prevent. Doing the last rung first would make the trust claims unverifiable while producing a demo that looks further along than it is.

Triggers that reopen the design

Not every change is a feature. These move a boundary, and each one requires the threat model to be revisited:

  • A host, domain, TLS terminator or reverse proxy.
  • More than one backend worker, which invalidates in-process rate limiting and inline parsing assumptions.
  • A real document, real Aadhaar number or real portal credential.
  • Key rotation, a KMS or HSM, object storage, OCR, email, analytics, error reporting or an AI API — each is a new processor or a new store.
  • A browser extension, portal adapter, or any runtime call to a portal.
  • Authentication changes: passwords, recovery flows, a second factor, or any staff role.
  • A new module or a new route family, which is where cross-account exposure tends to appear.
  • A dependency advisory affecting shipped code, or a re-pinned base image.

19Repository and running it

One repository, two applications, one catalog, a handful of scripts. Everything needed to run the product is checked in.

Layout

PathWhat lives there
apps/webThe Next.js progressive web app (TypeScript): vault, profile, catalog, drafts, locker, consent.
backendThe FastAPI application, its modules, Alembic migrations, and the test suites.
catalogVersioned service definitions and form templates — data, not code.
infra/composeThe one-command demo stack: PostgreSQL, backend and web, with every port bound to loopback.
docsArchitecture decision records, the compliance drafts, evaluation notes, planning records.
scriptsBootstrap, development, quality-gate and smoke-test commands.

The three commands that matter

scripts/bootstrap.sh    # create the Python environment, install both dependency trees
scripts/check.sh        # lint, type checks, tests and a production build for both apps
scripts/dev.sh          # backend on :8000, web on :3000, with dev sign-in enabled

check.sh is the same gate CI runs on every change. It is deliberately one command: if a change cannot pass one gate locally, it should not reach a reviewer.

The demo stack

infra/compose brings up PostgreSQL, the backend and the web app with a single compose command, waits for health, and a smoke script walks the whole citizen flow against it: sign in, consent, upload, extraction, promotion, draft, evidence, export, case. The stack is a demo, not the pilot: it uses synthetic documents, enables development sign-in so no authenticator is needed, and every port is loopback-only.

Configuration rules

  • Development sign-in is off unless explicitly enabled, and refused outside development.
  • Session cookies are marked Secure outside development; configuration refuses to start if the two disagree.
  • Limits, session lifetime and CORS origins are configuration, with safe defaults in code.
  • Local development uses SQLite; the compose stack and the pilot use PostgreSQL through the same migrations.

Contributing safely

Changes arrive as small pull requests with a test that fails first, the gate green, and the compliance documents updated in the same change if a claim in them became false. Synthetic data only, in code, tests, issues and demos. Security findings go through private reporting, not a public issue.

20How it is evaluated

The product is judged by whether a person can finish the packet unaided and trust what they see — not by how impressive the demo looks.

The session design

  1. Interview before demo. Ask about the participant's last attempt at the same certificate before showing the product, so their account is not anchored by what they just saw.
  2. Consent, separately. Notes, optional recording, and support messages are each their own choice; declining recording does not exclude anyone.
  3. The participant drives. The facilitator does not click. If someone stalls, the stall is recorded before help is offered, because the stall is the finding.
  4. Close with the hard questions. Where would this help, where would it fail, what would you not trust it with, would you use it again, would you pay.

The four measures

MeasureDefinition
Unaided completionThe participant reaches the export screen with no facilitator input.
Time on taskMinutes from sign-in to export, compared against a manual baseline of gathering and filling the same packet by hand.
TrustWhether they would use it for their own application, and whether they would pay — qualitative, not scored away.
The blocker listEvery stall, misread label and abandoned step, ranked by severity. A session that ends in a blocker is a valid result, not a failure to coach past.

Stop conditions

If a real document is offered, the session stops, nothing is uploaded, and anything already uploaded for that participant is deleted on request, recorded as an incident without personal data. If portal credentials, an OTP or a CAPTCHA are mentioned, RINAVI refuses, explains the boundary, and records the request as product feedback — it is a signal about what people want, and a reminder of what the product will not do about it.

What the pilot cannot prove

Extraction accuracy on real scans, minutes actually saved on the portal, conversion or willingness to pay at scale, and anything about portal behaviour. Those need real documents, which need the ladder in chapter 18.
